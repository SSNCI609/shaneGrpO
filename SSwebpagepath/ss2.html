Farming Methods and the Land

Different farming methods have different impacts on soil structure, erosion and nutrient cycles.

Intensive use of chemical fertilisers and pesticides can damage biodiversity above and below ground.

Sustainable approaches reduce these pressures by working with natural processes instead of against them.

Working With Nature

Agroforestry, crop rotation and mixed farming systems can support wildlife, improve resilience and keep soils productive for future generations.

Supporting Local Ecosystems

Planting hedgerows, leaving field margins and protecting wetlands all provide habitats for native species.

Pollinators such as bees, butterflies and other insects benefit from diverse flowering plants.

In return, these species help farmers by improving pollination and natural pest control.

The Role of Policy and Consumers

Government schemes and regulations can encourage farmers to protect habitats and reduce emissions.

Certification labels and local food networks help consumers choose more sustainable options.

Together, these choices shape landscapes and help deliver the aims of SDG 15: Life on Land.
